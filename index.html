          API cheat sheet
          
        
            
        Called once per simulated minute (1,440 times). Return setpoints in MW. Anything you leave out keeps its last value. Your score is societal cost: fuel + start-ups + $10,000 per MWh of blackout + $100 per tonne of CO₂ (lower is better). Unknown keys and non-numbers are ignored, with a warning in the console below naming the key.
        

        return {
  coal: 1200, gas: 900, peaker: 0,   // thermal targets (units start/stop automatically)
  commit: { gas: 4 },                // optional: keep at least N units online
  wind: 3000, solar: 3500,           // curtailment caps
  battery: -300,                     // + discharge, - charge (auto frequency response adds on top)
  dr: true                           // call demand response (limited calls)
}
        
            
        state: t (minute 0..1439), frequency, deviation, demand, generation, imbalance, fleets.{nuclear,coal,gas,peaker} (output, online, starting, capacity, onlineMW, unitMW, rampPerMin, startMin, minFrac, cost, co2), fleets.{wind,solar} (available, output, capacity), fleets.battery (soc, socFrac, mwh, mw), dr (callsLeft, active, pending), forecast.{demand,wind,solar}[minute].
        
            
        memory is yours to keep anything between calls. console.log works. Each call gets 250 ms; loop forever and the run stops, not the page.

        How the grid works
        
      
        Power has to be made the instant it is used. When generation falls short of demand, the spinning machines on the grid slow down and frequency drops below 60 Hz. Too much generation and it rises.
        
      
          
        
        • Below 59.3 Hz load is shed, 5% of customers at a time. (59.3 Hz is ERCOT's first automatic stage; in real life rolling blackouts are usually ordered by operators.)
          • 
        
        • Above 60.5 Hz a generator trips. That is a game rule to punish overshooting; real plants ride through much higher frequencies. From 60.3 Hz the dial counts down the headroom.
          • 
        
        • You are the governor. There is no automatic generator response, so frequency keeps drifting until you act. Only the battery reacts by itself, until it runs empty or full.
          • 
        
        • Plants are slow. Hot-start times: coal 4 h, gas 1 h, peakers 10 min. Nuclear doesn't move.
          • 
        
        • Score = fuel + start-ups + $10,000 per MWh of blackout + $100 per tonne of CO₂. Lower is better. Any blackout caps your grade; otherwise the grade compares your score with par. With carbon priced in, gas costs less per MWh than coal.
